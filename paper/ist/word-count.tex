\section*{Word-count declaration}

Manuscript: \emph{A Comparability Protocol for Benchmarking Relational Database Access Layers in Express.js} (submission build \texttt{ist\_main.tex}).

The official IST journal policy confirms a \textbf{15,000-word maximum for research papers}. The current author guidance supplied with this revision additionally assigns \textbf{200 words to each figure or table} and counts references and manuscript appendices. This declaration uses a deliberately conservative implementation of that rule.

\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{ll}
\hline
Component & Count \\
\hline
Seven body sections (\texttt{texcount} sum, including section headings and caption text encountered in those files) & 9,657 \\
Structured abstract (\texttt{texcount} sum) & 227 \\
Remaining front matter and required declarations (title/keywords, CRediT, competing interest, data availability, funding, and AI-use disclosures) & 595 \\
Main-text tables and figures (7 $\times$ 200) & 1,400 \\
Rendered reference list (65 entries; conservative \texttt{pdftotext} count including the heading and page folios) & 2,000 \\
\textbf{Conservative IST submission equivalent} & \textbf{13,879} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}\end{center}

The controlling total is therefore \textbf{13,879 words}, leaving \textbf{1,121 words of headroom} under the 15,000-word limit. The structured abstract contains 222 prose words (227 under the \texttt{texcount} sum convention), below 300 words.

This total is intentionally stricter than a research-content-only count. It includes all required declaration text even though such text is commonly treated as submission metadata. It also retains caption words already encountered by \texttt{texcount} and then assigns the full 200-word allowance to every rendered float, so it does not undercount captions. The paper has no manuscript appendices. The separately submitted \texttt{supplement.pdf} is online supplementary material, not an appendix embedded in the research paper.
